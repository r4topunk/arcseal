Timelock-encrypted voting · live on Arc mainnet

Every vote stays sealed until the clock opens it.

ArcSeal locks each vote to a future drand round. Nobody can read it while voting is open — not the other members, not the contract. When the round arrives, anyone can open every vote at once.

What the contract promises, and keeps.

SealedDAO is immutable: no owner, no upgrade, no pause. Treasury token, quorum and reveal payment are fixed at deployment.

The hash rules

The contract never parses a ciphertext and runs no onchain BLS check. It only compares hashes at reveal.

No relayer

Any visitor or SDK script can reveal. Nothing in the protocol depends on this site.

Bad items never block

Mismatched, unknown or duplicate items are skipped (RevealSkipped); the rest of the batch still lands.

Pull-only payouts

Payouts are credited, then claimed by their own recipient. A blocklisted address stalls no one.

Quorum ≠ outcome

Quorum counts sealed votes; passing counts revealed ones only. Ties fail. An unrevealed vote is never lost.

No drain without a pass

The reveal payment is credited only for a proposal that met quorum, so a lone member cannot empty the treasury.

Built for Arc

Why Arc.

Arc has no VRF and no timelock service, so ArcSeal brings its own primitive — and leans on what Arc does have.

Gas paid in USDC
Sealing a vote costs about 0.0014 USDC; the reveal payment is paid in the same unit the revealer spends.
Deterministic finality
A sealed vote is final on inclusion, so it counts right after the close with no confirmation depth.
Native USDC treasury
Treasury and gas are the same asset, read through the 6-decimal ERC-20 view at 0x3600…0000.
USDC blocklist
Pull-based claim() means a blocked recipient can never stall a proposal for anyone else.
Privacy, stated plainly

Secret during voting. Public, per address, forever after. This is not anonymity.

Voting open · nobody can read any vote
Anyone can decrypt · (voter, choice) public→ ∞
propose▲ close round N — reveal, finalize, execute
  • Secrecy holds during voting. Nobody — not other voters, not the contract, not ArcSeal's authors — can read a sealed vote's choice before its round is public. This relies on drand's League of Entropy threshold staying honest.
  • From the close round on, anyone can decrypt every sealed vote from the chain; after the reveal, each vote is public per address, permanently, in the event log.
  • This is not anonymity. If you need who voted, or how, to stay hidden after the fact, ArcSeal is not the right tool.
  • It does not stop a willing seller. Your vote receipt proves your choice to anyone you show it to, so ArcSeal is not coercion-resistant.
